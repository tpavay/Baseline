Baseline · exercise taxonomy — v2 (reworked)

Rethinking the model so it works in every case.

You were right to push back. This version fixes the category muddle, separates intent from the exercise entirely, adds the axis every real database has and we were missing (equipment), and is grounded in how the open exercise databases actually model this.

PLAN v2 · NOT CODE

01 What you flagged — and the principle behind it

Three problems, one root cause: my first cut mixed together things that are actually independent.

✕
"Category vs tags — what's the difference?"
Fair — my "Category" (cycling/running/erg/strength/carry…) was secretly doing three jobs at once: equipment, movement, and how-you-log. That's why it blurred into "tags."
✕
"What if they squat for hypertrophy, not strength?"
This is the big one. Strength vs hypertrophy is not a property of the exercise — it's how you program it (sets × reps × load × RPE), which Baseline already stores on the prescription. So "strength" never belonged on the exercise.
✕
"Sled push is not a carry."
Correct. It got mis-slotted because the old category forced one bucket. Under clean axes: equipment = sled, pattern = push/drive, muscles = quads/glutes. No "carry."
The principle: an exercise definition describes only what is intrinsic and permanent about the movement — what it works, how the body does it, what gear it needs. Everything about why or how hard (strength, hypertrophy, endurance, a specific session) is programming, and lives on the prescription, not the exercise. Keep those two worlds apart and the whole thing works in every case.

02 How the established databases model it

You asked me to look it up. Here's the consensus across the open datasets and ExRx — they're remarkably aligned, and two findings directly shape our design.

SourceAxes they store
Free Exercise DB
800+ exercises, the open standard		 muscles (primary/secondary) · equipment · force (push/pull/static) · mechanic (compound/isolation) · level (beginner→expert) · category (strength/cardio/stretching/plyometrics/powerlifting/strongman/olympic)
wger
open-source, popular		 muscles (primary/secondary) · equipment · category (a muscle-region / discipline mix)
ExRx.net
the reference for classification		 muscles · utility (basic/auxiliary) · mechanic (compound/isolation) · force (push/pull/static)

Two findings that shape our model

1. Not one of them stores training intent. There is no "hypertrophy vs strength" field anywhere — because it's programming, not classification. (When the Free DB says category: "strength" it means "resistance training," as opposed to cardio/stretching — not "training for strength.") This is your hypertrophy point, confirmed by every source.

2. Their single "category" field is itself a muddle — it crams modality (cardio, stretching), discipline (powerlifting, strongman, olympic), and method (plyometrics) into one enum. Baseline can be cleaner than the standard by splitting those into separate, honest axes.

Bottom line: the durable, universal axes are muscles + equipment + force + mechanic. We adopt those, upgrade "force" to a richer movement-pattern axis, and replace the muddled "category" with a clean split of how-you-log (already have) and discipline tags.

Sources: Free Exercise DB (schema) · wger REST API · ExRx classification glossary

★ How Strava does it — and they just shipped muscle maps

Good call. Two things stand out, both backing this direction — and one is brand new (Strava's May 2026 strength overhaul).

Activity-level "sport types" (~50)

Strava labels a whole activity with one sport type — Run, Ride, Swim, WeightTraining, Crossfit, HIIT… and notably Elliptical, StairStepper, Rowing are first-class (with Ride / EBikeRide / VirtualRide as a cycling family). That's the coarse cousin of our modality + specific cardio equipment — and it confirms naming cardio machines specifically instead of "machine."

Muscle maps (new, May 2026)

Every strength activity now auto-generates a front + back muscle map from the exercises performed, shown in place of the route map, alongside a sets/reps/weight log. That's exactly our §09 payoff — and a strong hint for where it lives.

Where we differ (in our favor): Strava tags cardio at the activity level (one sport type per session) and only drills into exercises for strength. Baseline is exercise-level throughout, with muscles + pattern + equipment per movement — a superset that produces Strava's muscle map and the push/pull balance and "biked this week" analytics Strava can't.

Sources: Strava strength overhaul + muscle maps · Strava supported sport types

  2. Map their fields into our model — a one-time mapping layer: their 17 muscles → our 22 (we add obliques / hip flexors / split delts), their coarse push/pull "force" → our patterns, their muddled "category" → our modality + tags. Lossy in our favor (we're richer), so expect an enrichment pass.

The taxonomy work in this plan is exactly what makes a clean import possible.

03 The reworked Baseline model

A handful of independent axes describe the exercise. Intent is deliberately not among them.

NEW

Muscles — primary / secondary

What it works. Powers the body heatmap. Optional (cardio has none).

Quadriceps, Glutes … (22 values)

NEW

Equipment

What gear it needs. The axis every DB has and we lacked — drives "what can I do with a kettlebell," substitutions, and import matching.

barbell · dumbbell · kettlebell · machine · sled · erg …

NEW

Movement pattern

How the body organizes the effort — a richer replacement for the DBs' push/pull "force." 1–2 per exercise (a thruster is squat + push).

squat · hinge · lunge · push · pull · carry · rotation · gait · hold

NEW

Mechanic — compound / isolation

Standard, cheap, useful ("enough compound work?"). Straight from ExRx / Free DB.

compound · isolation

NEW

Level

Beginner / intermediate / expert — inherited from the seed DB, not hand-graded. Fuels "suggest me something easier" later.

beginner · intermediate · expert

NEW

Modality

What kind of training it is — one small explicit field so cardio is first-class and totalable, not guessed.

resistance · cardio · hold · mobility

HAVE

Logging metrics (what you record)

Already modeled as supported / defaults metrics. Says what you log; modality says what kind it is.

reps, load · duration, distance, pace, power

NEW

Discipline tags

Cross-cutting, non-exclusive labels. What the muddled "category" was reaching for. HYROX lives here.

HYROX · Olympic · Calisthenics · Mobility · Plyometric

NOT ON THE EXERCISE

Intent — strength / hypertrophy / endurance / power

Expressed by the prescription (sets × reps × load × RPE × tempo × rest), which Baseline already stores on the workout. A back squat at 5×3 @ 90% is strength; 4×12 @ 65% is hypertrophy — same exercise, same muscles, same everything above.

04 "Category vs tags," resolved

The honest answer to your confusion: we delete the vague "category." What it was trying to say splits cleanly, and only one piece is "tags."

Modality — one small explicit field

What kind of training it is. Set on each exercise (mapped from the DB's category on import), so cardio is first-class and totalable — not guessed.

  • Resistance — reps/load work
  • Cardio — bike, run, row, swim, elliptical, stairs…
  • Hold / isometric · Mobility

Tags — cross-cutting, chosen

What discipline or community an exercise belongs to. Non-exclusive (an exercise can be several), and never about mechanics or measurement.

  • HYROX — spans running, erg, sled, wall balls…
  • Olympic weightlifting, Calisthenics, Mobility…
  • You filter/collect by these; they don't change the metrics.
Rule of thumb: if it changes what you log or how the body moves, it's an intrinsic axis (equipment / pattern / metrics). If it's a label for grouping that a marketer or coach would use, it's a tag.

Equipment — DB gear + functional/HYROX + specific cardio machines

On your bike question — good one. The databases are strength-first: the Free DB has no cardio equipment at all, so a bike/treadmill is just "machine." We go more specific on purpose — naming Bike, Rower, Treadmill, Stair stepper, etc. — so "how much did I bike this week" is a one-line filter (sum duration where equipment = Bike). One deliberate simplification: Bike covers stationary / outdoor / echo / Concept2 bike-erg — they're all "a bike" for time totals; the finer differences (erg power, etc.) live on the exercise's identity + metrics, not on equipment. Same for the rest.

Movement pattern — subsumes the DBs' push/pull/static "force"

SquatHingeLungePushPullCarryRotationGaitHold / isometric

Good catch on "gait." Gait = bipedal locomotion — running, walking, treadmill. Not cycling. And pattern is optional, exactly like muscles: a bike is cyclic leg cardio with no discrete strength pattern, so it simply gets none. Ergs that genuinely have a pattern keep it — a rower is a hinge + pull, a ski erg is a pull. Forcing "gait" onto a bike was the same error as tagging a run's "quads," and this plan now avoids it.

Note: kettlebell is equipment, not a tag — a common conflation the databases make. "Kettlebell sport" would be a tag; the bell itself is gear.

Level — inherited from the seed DB, not hand-graded

BeginnerIntermediateExpert

You asked how we'd grade this — the honest answer is we mostly don't grade it ourselves. "Inherited" just means: that public database already has a beginner / intermediate / expert label on each of its 800+ exercises (its authors set them), so when we import an exercise it brings its level along — we don't invent a rulebook. Your instincts match their conventions anyway: a bodyweight squat or bike is beginner, a barbell back squat or thruster is intermediate, an Olympic clean is expert. For our current 51 (which aren't from that DB) and any custom exercise, I'll just pick a sensible default from equipment + mechanic — bodyweight/machine → easier, barbell/olympic → harder — and you can change any of them.

It currently drives a placeholder glyph and some filtering. Post-rework the glyph comes from equipment/pattern, and filtering gets better (by equipment, muscle, pattern, or tag). This is the one part with real migration cost — flagged in the build.

09 The payoff — and where it lives

Muscles → a body heatmap. Patterns → a push/pull/hinge balance read. Modality → cardio-minutes. Equipment → "what can I train with what I have" + smarter import matching. Here's where you said it should surface:

Home — your week at a glance

A weekly distribution summary on the home screen: muscle balance + cardio-minutes for the current week, so you see coverage without digging.

Profile — you + your workouts

Profile holds your info / preferences, with Settings behind a gear icon (its own view, not a tab). A Workouts section/toggle lists every workout (title + actual amounts); open one and it shows the front/back muscle map when it has strength exercises — like Strava's per-workout map.

Cardio, made concrete: cardio is first-class via the modality field, so "142 min of cardio this week" falls right out — the sum of logged duration where modality == cardio (bike, treadmill, elliptical, stairs, walk, run, row, swim), none hard-coded. Same trick gives resistance volume, mobility minutes, etc.

These two surfaces are real navigation work (Profile is currently a settings stub). I'd prototype them in Lavish against the design system before building — separate from, and enabled by, the taxonomy in this plan.
